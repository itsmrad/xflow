<Steps>
<Step title="Put your cursor somewhere">
Any text field: your editor, a terminal prompt, a browser, a chat box.
</Step>
<Step title="Hold Fn and talk">
Capture starts the moment you press. After 150 ms the start sound plays and the pill appears above the Dock.

<Pill state="listening" />
</Step>
<Step title="Let go">
The pill switches to processing while the provider transcribes.

<Pill state="processing" />

The text lands at your cursor, with a trailing space so you can keep going.
</Step>
</Steps>

## Watch it happen

In another terminal, follow the daemon's events:

```sh
xflow watch --json
```

A normal dictation moves through `listening`, `processing` and `success`. The final event carries the text, how it was delivered and the timings. See [Events](/docs/reference/events) for the fields.

## When the text doesn't appear

xflow keeps the transcript before it tries to deliver it, so a failed paste never loses your words.

- If the app or window changed, or the field isn't editable, the text is copied instead. The pill says “Copied — press ⌘V to paste”.
- Password fields refuse dictation.
- Multi-line text is never pasted into a terminal automatically; it stays on the clipboard.

```sh
xflow last        # print the last transcript
xflow copy        # copy it again
xflow paste       # insert it into the focused app
```

## When nothing was heard

Recordings shorter than 300 ms are discarded without uploading. A recording that is silent, or only a click, ends with “Didn't catch anything” instead of a guessed sentence.

## Music or a call in the background

Turn on `recording.ignore_speakers` (off by default) to keep sound playing on the computer out of your dictation:

```sh
xflow config set recording.ignore_speakers true
```

On macOS this records through Apple's voice processing, the echo canceller FaceTime uses, and falls back to the plain microphone if that can't start or `recording.device` names a specific microphone. It can take a moment longer to start. On Linux, make the PipeWire or PulseAudio echo-cancel source your default input; `xflow doctor` shows how.

## Dictate into a pipe instead

`xflow listen` prints the final text to stdout and doesn't touch the desktop:

```sh
xflow listen --once --seconds 5 | pbcopy
```
