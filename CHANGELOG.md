# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Dictation
- **Spoken underscore and dash work in every app.** In a browser, chat app or Electron app, "EBS underscore 321 underscore GHJ" becomes `EBS_321_GHJ` and "Hi dash There" becomes `Hi-There`. Outside terminals and editors a dash joins only IDs (capitals, digits, single letters, camelCase) or two capitalised words; "a dash of salt", "the 100 meter dash today", "underscore the point" and "Dash" as a name stay as said. Dot, slash, colon, operators and quotes stay in terminals and editors. Whisper's commas around the word no longer block it: "Hi, Dash, There." becomes `Hi-There.` `formatting.spoken_code = false` turns it all off.
- **"cloud.ai" becomes Claude.ai.** Bare "cloud" is unchanged.

### Learning from corrections
- **Corrections are saved seconds after you make them.** About two seconds after a fixed word settles and your cursor moves off it, or as soon as you switch fields or send, instead of at the end of a 60-second watch. The notice names the word for four seconds with a clickable Undo, even while you record.
- **The next dictation is right.** An unusual word fixed to a close spelling now saves a literal correction at once, so it no longer depends on the recognizer honoring hints, including after model cleanup. An everyday word fixed to an unusual one saves only the word until you make the same fix again.
- **More fixes count.** Ordinary typos (`definately`), short messages, whole retypes and fixes right after the paste are learned, in Chrome and Safari fields too, where a trailing space used to stop learning and leave your clipboard unrestored.
- **The notice no longer swallows keys.** Its helper could become the front app when launched while you were idle, sending your typing nowhere until it exited.
- **Deleting a word deletes its corrections**, and says how many.
- **Tighter reads.** Field text is read in bounded ranges behind a security and focus check before every read, and watching stops for good on a real focus change.
- **See what was saved, and take it back.** On macOS, when xflow learns a word from your correction, the pill shows it for one second with an Undo ×; `xflow dictionary undo` takes the latest save back afterwards, once, until config.toml changes or xflowd restarts. Undo restores the file exactly as it was and never blocks the word; `xflow dictionary remove` still does that.
- **Short messages learn names.** Retyping a name in a short message (`Rimpas or Kar, call me` → `Rimpa Sarkar, call me`) is learned, and four misheard words may become a one- or two-word name. Replies that are only the name, three words or fewer, are still not learned.
- **Correction watching keeps its Chromium accessibility setup.** If finding the edited field turns on an app's accessibility, xflow keeps that setting until correction watching ends, then restores it.
- **Spoken line breaks stay intact.** Manual multi-word replacements and phrase fillers no longer consume text across a spoken “new line” or “new paragraph”.
- **Names you keep correcting stick better.** Correcting a word that's already saved moves it to the front of the hint list. Correcting a narrow misheard phrase to a name already in the dictionary may add that exact phrase as a replacement, for 2–3 plain words only; a single word like "cloud" is never rewritten, and a comma or line break inside the phrase leaves it alone. Hints still can't guarantee the next transcript.
- **Fewer truncated saves.** A change folded into Enter, or arriving just after the field empties, now learns nothing; search boxes and address bars are no longer watched. Apps that duplicate their edit notifications can therefore skip learning on Enter; history corrections remain available. `xflow doctor` shows learning counts and whether xflowd's dictionary matches config.toml, without any words.
- **No lost edits.** Every xflow command that writes config.toml, the TUI and xflowd now take the same lock, and successful reloads and ordered daemon writes keep the in-memory dictionary current; a reload refused during recording is reported and must be retried.

## 0.2.1-beta — 2026-11-02

### Recording pill
- **The pill only speaks up when something is wrong.** It shows while you talk and collapses the moment you release the key or stop; pasting and copying happen silently, with no "Done", "Pasted — check it landed" or "Discarded". A failure shows a red pill for at most 2 s with a short reason: "Couldn't transcribe", "No speech heard", "Couldn't paste", "Recording failed", or "Copied — paste with ⌘V" (Ctrl+V on Linux) when the text could not be pasted. An asked-for copy is not a failure. `xflow watch`, `xflow status` and history still carry the full outcome. On GNOME the success duration setting is gone and the failure duration is capped at 2 s. (#30)
- **"Can't hear you — check mic".** If nothing has been heard 2 s after you start, the pill turns amber with this hint; it clears at the first sound, and recording continues. (#30)
- **"Recording stops in 1 min".** One minute before `recording.max_seconds`, the pill and `xflow watch` warn you. (#30)

### Microphone
- **Bluetooth headphones stay in high quality (macOS).** When your default input is a Bluetooth headset, xflow records with the built-in mic instead, so the headphones keep playing in stereo rather than dropping to call quality. Turn it off with `recording.avoid_bluetooth = false`. A mic you pin by name (`recording.device`, see `xflow devices`) is always used, Bluetooth included. If the pinned mic is missing, xflow uses the automatic choice and says so in `xflow status`. (#96)
- **Unplugging the mic mid-dictation keeps what you said.** xflow stops within about a second, transcribes the audio captured before it, and reports "microphone disconnected". The microphone is fully released after every stop. (#96)

### Dictation
- **Preferred languages, including Hindi and English in one breath.** Set `stt.languages = ["hi", "en"]` (or another set your model supports) and xflow tells the speech service to expect both. Hindi stays in Devanagari and English terms in Latin script. Models that can't combine languages are refused before you record. (#40)
- **Spoken symbols in terminals and editors.** Next to code-like words, "user underscore id" becomes `user_id`, "dash dash verbose" becomes `--verbose`, and "dot slash build dot sh" becomes `./build.sh`; arrows, colons, quotes and brackets work too. Ordinary speech like "a dash of salt" or "plus one" stays as said, and prose apps are unchanged.
- **Clock times read as times.** "Meet at ten thirty" now gives "10:30" instead of "10.30". Prices, versions and decimals keep their dot.
- **A soft first word is no longer trimmed away,** and "new line" no longer leaves stray commas behind ("First line,\n, second line").
- **Opt in: hold over selected text to edit it (macOS).** Turn on Settings › Writing › Edit selected text (`cleanup.rewrite_selection = true`; off by default). Select "Tuesday", hold the key and say "Monday", and the selection becomes "Monday"; say "make this a bulleted list" or "translate to German" and it is rewritten. The selected text is sent to your cleanup provider, so it needs one and never runs in raw mode. With nothing selected, in hands-free, or when you say more than 12 words (that is new text, pasted as said), the hold is normal dictation. Terminals, search boxes, address bars and password fields always get normal dictation, and so does a selection over 6,000 characters, with a notice. If the rewrite fails, is slow, reads like a reply, or the selection changed meanwhile, your selection is left as it was and the text is copied. Reading the selection runs alongside the microphone start, so dictation starts no later. History records the rewrite.
- **Mis-heard product and domain terms come out spelled right, and ordinary English is never rewritten.** About 260 shipped terms fix mis-hearings that are not English words: developer and AI names ("kube ctl" → kubectl, "postgress" → Postgres, "vercell" → Vercel, "nextjs" → Next.js, "shadkin" → shadcn), business terms ("soc2" → SOC 2), spreadsheet functions ("zlookup" → XLOOKUP) and finance terms ("ebida" → EBITDA, "pnl" → P&L). A spelling that could be ordinary speech changes only next to a technical word ("ask cloud to review" → "ask Claude to review", "the rust toolchain" → Rust); "rust on the car", "app store", "ssh, the baby is asleep" and names like Lara or Ios stay as you said them, and a capitalised "SWIFT" or "Swift" keeps its case. Developer and business terms are on everywhere, spreadsheet terms in Excel, Numbers and LibreOffice Calc, and finance terms when you turn them on with `xflow config set vocab.finance true`. No network call; it adds under 0.1 ms to a 350-word dictation. Your dictionary words and removed words always win. The single-word context corrections from #105 for Cursor, PostgreSQL, npm, pnpm, GitLab, OAuth, JSON, SQLite, Kafka, Terraform, PyTorch, TypeScript, tRPC, gRPC and WebSocket are removed under this stricter rule; recall for those terms is meant to come from recognition hints sent to the speech service instead.
- **Smart formatting is on after setup.** When your speech key can also run cleanup (Groq today), setup turns on light cleanup with that key. It turns spoken sequences into lists, keeps only your correction when you say "actually" or "no wait", writes times and amounts as digits, and spells your dictionary terms. The setup screen says so: "Smart formatting: on · uses your Groq key · ~+0.2 s". Turn it off in Settings › Writing. Configs where you already set cleanup up are not changed. (#67)
- **Dictations fit where they land (macOS).** xflow reads a few characters on each side of the cursor and adds a space or doesn't. Dictating "That we should." after "I think" gives "I think that we should." At the start of a field, after a new line, or after a sentence's period and a space, the first letter becomes a capital. Mid-sentence, only small words like "that", "the" or "so" are lowercased; names, "Notes", "Monday", "OK", "I", ALLCAPS, file names and your dictionary words keep their case. "Dr.", "e.g." or a period with no space after it don't start a sentence. Password fields, terminals and fields it can't identify are never read, and what it reads stays on your Mac. If the field hasn't answered by the time your text is ready, the text goes in unchanged. Turn it off with `injection.smart_spacing = false`. (#24)
- **Thousands of cloud, DevOps, system design and CS terms are recognised.** The shipped hint list grew from about 300 to about 3,000 names (AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Kubernetes and DevOps tools, databases, system design, algorithms and ML), each confirmed on an official product page, its documentation or Wikipedia. Each dictation still sends only the most relevant ones: terms you dictated recently come first, then the area they belong to (say AWS after you mention EKS), then the focused app's list. Window-title words can steer the area but are never sent. Nothing is rewritten, and it adds no time when you stop.
- **Product and industry terms are heard right.** xflow now tells the speech model about common developer and business terms (Claude, kubectl, Supabase, Vercel, Salesforce), spreadsheet functions in Excel, Numbers and LibreOffice Calc (XLOOKUP, SUMIFS), and finance terms if you turn them on (`vocab.finance = true`). Terminals and editors get developer terms first. Your dictionary words always go first, and nothing is rewritten after transcription. In a 27-clip test on Groq, term recall went from 44% to 73% for about 12 ms more per dictation. These are public product names, not your data. Turn it off with `stt.domain_hints = false`. ElevenLabs, Deepgram and AssemblyAI get your words only, since they bill keyterms extra. If a provider rejects the shipped terms, xflow retries once with your words only.
- **Cleanup never answers you.** A question or "ignore your instructions" in your dictation stays text. If the model answers, rewrites, drops a path or flag, or takes longer than about 3 s, xflow pastes your words as transcribed and says why. Dictations of six words or fewer, mostly-code text and terminals skip cleanup, so they cost no time. (#67, #68)

### Privacy
- **The focused app's name is no longer sent to the writing service by default.** Turn it on in Settings › Privacy › Share app name. If you had set `cleanup.app_context = true` yourself, it stays on. (#38)
- **Opt in: spell the names in your editor's title bar (macOS).** With Settings › Privacy › Use window titles (`privacy.window_terms = true`, off by default; macOS only for now), xflow reads the focused code editor's or terminal app's window title when you start dictating and picks out file, project and branch names. With `useTaskStore.ts — myapp` open, "open use task store" becomes `open useTaskStore` and "use task store dot ts" becomes `useTaskStore.ts`. A camelCase name of three or more parts from the title is used only when you say its exact words, and not right after "the", "a", "my" and similar words; other title names are used only as a file name said with "dot". Only editor and terminal apps are read: never browsers (even a web terminal), mail or password managers, never a password field. The title is never sent to a provider, saved or logged; a name you dictate becomes part of your text like any other word. A title that takes longer than 100 ms is skipped. (#65)
- **File names you say with "dot" match your dictionary.** "user service dot py" becomes `user_service.py` when that file name is in your dictionary words or `stt.vocabulary`, with or without window titles; underscores and hyphens don't need to be said. (#65)

## 0.2.0-beta — 2026-10-29

Beta for macOS 13+ (universal: Apple silicon and Intel; `.pkg` or Homebrew) and Linux (tarball).

The macOS binaries are signed with xflow's own long-lived certificate, not an Apple Developer ID, and are **not notarised** (Developer ID is pending). When you first run `xflow`, setup asks macOS for Accessibility and Microphone for `xflowd`, and asks for Input Monitoring only if your shortcut key can't be heard without it. Because every future release is signed with the same certificate, those permissions carry over to `.pkg` updates. A Homebrew upgrade may ask again until xflow ships as an app bundle (#19). Install from a terminal with `sudo installer -pkg xflow-0.2.0-beta.pkg -target /`. A `.pkg` downloaded in a browser is blocked until you allow it in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway**.

### Dictation
- **20-minute dictations.** Long recordings are cut into 30–60 s segments at a pause and transcribed while you are still talking, so stopping a 12-minute dictation gives you text in about 0.6 s. If one segment fails, it is retried once and the rest is still delivered, with a warning. The default limit goes from 2 to 20 minutes, and the cleanup step no longer truncates long text. (#85)
- **Your developer terms come out spelled right.** Words in your dictionary and vocabulary, including the ones xflow learned from your corrections, are restored after transcription, so "next js" becomes `Next.js`. This runs locally, after transcription and before cleanup. In a 156-sentence prose test it changed 0 words that should stay as they are. Turn it off with `formatting.snap_to_dictionary = false`. (#82)
- **More dictionary hints reach the speech model.** The hint list is now measured in tokens against Whisper's 224-token limit (up to 200 estimated tokens) instead of 224 bytes, which sends 1.8× more terms. Exact dev-term recall goes from 47% to 53% on the default model and from 50% to 61% on large-v3. (#89)
- **Spoken lists become lists.** "three roles one developer two designer three manager" becomes a numbered Markdown list. "First… second…" and "bullet point…" work too. This runs locally and works without AI cleanup. (#81)
- **Optional: ignore sound from your speakers** while dictating, using macOS echo cancellation: `xflow config set recording.ignore_speakers true`. It is off by default. (#58)

### macOS
- **Paste is about 40 ms faster.** The clipboard is read and written in-process instead of by running `pbcopy`/`pbpaste` five times per paste. (#88)
- **Plain `xflow` opens setup.** It walks through only the steps that are missing and ends with a "hold Fn and say something" test. The installer no longer opens a Terminal window by itself. (#61)
- **No more Keychain password prompts.** Provider API keys now live in xflow's own encrypted store (a 0600 file in your config directory). A key saved by an older version moves over the first time you run `xflow key status` in a terminal, and macOS asks you to confirm once. Or re-enter it with `xflow key set <provider>`, with no prompt at all. (#80)

### Fixes
- If `xflow listen` or the TUI exits while recording (closed terminal, `kill -9`), the recording stops instead of running until the time limit. `xflow start` and `xflow toggle` still leave the recording running, as before. (#87)
- Fixed a flaky macOS daemon test. (#83)

### For contributors
- The repo is a monorepo: the Rust workspace is in `apps/cli` and the website in `apps/web`. (#71) The website design spec is in `docs/web/DESIGN.md`. (#72)
- Release packages include the licences of embedded third-party data: SCOWL word lists and OpenAI's BPE tables.
- Debug builds of `xflowd` can dictate from a WAV file (`XFLOW_TEST_AUDIO`), for QA without a microphone. (#79)
- The backend for xflow Pro (accounts, CLI device sign-in, billing, hosted transcription) is in `apps/web`. The CLI cannot sign in to it yet. (#78)
